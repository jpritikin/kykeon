## Drugs

Using drugs is a human right.[^hart] But a drug taken as a sacrament asks for more, because a sacrament is a promise about what happens inside a person. Ours promises to support the flourishing of society. The test of character below is how anyone can check that the promise is kept. The practice has three parts: personal healing, the circle, and the test.

Psychedelic culture has long hoped these substances were a shortcut to a better world: Dissolve enough egos and cruelty gives way to empathy. The evidence is mixed at best. People have profound experiences, feel connected to everything, and go back to work on Monday largely unchanged. Insight, it turns out, is not the same as the ability to act under pressure. What matters is not dissolving the ego, a term explained below, but the trust of its parts in the Self to lead.

So what can drugs offer a religion? To answer, we need to know what the Self is and what gets in its way.

## Psychology Basics

These ideas come from Internal Family Systems (IFS), a form of therapy developed by Richard Schwartz and taught by the [IFS Institute](https://ifs-institute.com).

**Parts.** The mind is not one voice. We all have *parts*: an inner critic, a planner, a frightened child, a protector who stashes food. Each has its own feelings and goals, and each is trying to help. Together, the parts are what people usually call the *ego*. Beneath the parts is the *Self*, a calm, curious, compassionate presence. Blended parts crowd it out, but it is always there to return to. *Self-leadership* means letting the Self, not a blended part, decide how you act. The parts are not the enemy. With Self-leadership, they can do their jobs in harmony.

**Blending and unblending.** When a part *blends* with you, you stop noticing it and start being it. You don't think "a part of me is afraid." You just are afraid, and the fear's view of the world is the only view. *Unblending* is stepping back so the part is something you can notice and talk to.

**Emotional distance and steering.** Blending is not a switch but a matter of distance. At zero distance you are the part. A little farther back, the part is loud but you can say what it is feeling. Farther still, it waits quietly while you attend to other things. Holding a part at a distance until the time is right is *mental steering*. Steering that holds under provocation is *firmness*. It works when even the loudest parts grudgingly trust you to listen.

**Mental injury.** When an experience feels too threatening for the whole mind to bear, the mind *splits*. The part that felt the experience is cut off from the rest. Other parts may work hard to keep it locked away. A child punished for anger may exile the anger. A teenager teased for crying may exile their sadness.

The exiled feelings don't vanish. They leak out as tension, sudden rage, anxiety, or a vague sense that something is missing. Healing means witnessing the exile with the Self present, which frees its protectors too.

## Entheogens

No drug that mimics the split of mental injury has a place in our practice. Such drugs produce behavior that the rest of the mind neither directs nor remembers, much like a part cut off from the whole. That is why we have no interest in datura[^datura] or in sleep aids like zolpidem.[^zolpidem] A sacrament should help you approach reality and the people around you.

The drugs that remain play three roles. *Tuning forks* sound one clean state, such as deep relaxation or non-duality, that you can learn to reproduce without the drug. *Empathogens* such as MDMA and kykeon ease the urge of parts to blend, so newcomers can practice compassion before it comes naturally. *Amplifiers* such as N,N-DMT and psilocybin raise Self-energy and the inclination to blend at once, which puts your steering under load. We see three opportunities for a sacrament:

**Personal emotional healing.** Exiled parts can be met and heard while the rest of the mind stays present. What was split off can rejoin. Kykeon can facilitate this process.

**The circle.** People who have been vulnerable together know one another differently. Kykeon, as an empathogen, can serve here too.

**A test of character.** The most important application. Leaders hold power over others, so the whole community depends on their character. Kykeon is the wrong cup for this, because it soothes the provocation. The test needs an amplifier, as explained below.

## An Uncheatable Test of Character

Knowing what's right and doing it under pressure are different things. Therapists feed their own need to be needed. Judges sentence from irritation. Police escalate because adrenaline is doing the thinking. Parents pass their unprocessed trauma to their children. Each holds power over the vulnerable. What's missing is unblending: noticing what you feel and choosing your response instead of letting the feeling drive. When that holds under provocation, we call it firmness.

The stakes rise with power. Before the Tenerife runway collision, Chernobyl, and the Challenger launch, someone's doubt, reluctance, or warning was already on record. What was missing was the firmness to heed it.

Firmness is easy to claim and hard to verify, so the question is how to measure it. Questionnaires capture vocabulary, not behavior. A trained observer's judgment is slow, expensive, and varies between observers. Daily life provokes at random, never the same way twice.

A better test would reliably stir up your own material and watch what happens next. Amplifiers such as N,N-DMT, psilocybin, and LSD do this, which is also why bad trips happen. Other drug classes are unsuitable. Dissociatives such as ketamine detach you from the experience. Empathogens such as MDMA and kykeon soothe the provocation. Stimulants such as cocaine manufacture confidence instead of testing it.

The psychedelic dose is measured, but what it stirs up is not. Fear, grandiosity, or greed may surge. Either you blend with it and are swept away, or your steering holds and you remain firm.[^firmeza] The outcome depends on how you meet it, which is what makes it a test. Because you stay present and remembering,[^dose] being swept away is a failure of unblending, not a loss of agency. The test can be repeated, so one stumble does not settle anything. Fail once and it might be the occasion. Fail repeatedly and it's you.

Taking the test is voluntary, and we do not propose making it a requirement for holding office. Those who aspire to lead can distinguish themselves by taking it, and the circle may weigh the difference. We ask only that power answer to evidence of conduct, such as how someone treats the people who depend on them and how they take criticism.

The test is a diagnostic, not a treatment. It does not confer integrity. It reveals whether someone already has it, and can guide the parts work that strengthens it. The test itself can be implemented by more than one tradition, which makes this a [modular religion](/modular/).

## The Role of Kykeon

The test demands courage: Material can ambush you while failure is witnessed. Courage like that needs a bedrock of trust.

Kykeon showers our parts with grace. Held in grace, parts can develop trust in the Self. Shared in a circle, kykeon also begins the bond of vulnerability that holds a community together. It is the church's welcome: an ancient cup offered at the door to all.

Kykeon is also the most extravagantly euphoric drug we know, which makes it the easiest to indulge. Without a purpose, it becomes the Friday-night couch on a grand scale. The test gives the euphoria a job.

## Mutual Support

The test and the wider political order can strengthen each other. The test is open to anyone who holds or seeks power over others. The church is the proving ground, not the throne. The wider order is where leaders serve.

Why not rely on institutional design alone?[^incorruptible] Every bylaw, mission lock, and ownership trust is enforceable only because a legal system backs it, and a legal system is an institution run by people who can look away. Structure moves the problem without solving it. The leverage point is whether the person holding power can feel the pull of self-interest, fear, or ambition and not go under.

Politics safeguards religious freedom: Without laws and officials who protect the right to gather, worship, and use sacraments, no church survives. But politics is only as trustworthy as the people who hold power. Those whose Self-leadership has held firm are better candidates for that trust. In turn, they protect the freedom that makes the test possible.

## Celebration

The Second Church of the Kykeon celebrates healing, which prepares us for the test of character, and community, which makes the test's results something we can act on.

One firm person steadies a household. One with power steadies everyone who depends on them. Doubts are voiced and heeded, a mistake is owned the day it is made, and the attention once spent guarding against betrayal can turn toward the sun. People can stop bracing and look up.
