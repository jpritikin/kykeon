## Drugs

Using drugs is a human right.[^hart] But religious use asks for more. Ours has three parts: personal healing, the circle, and a test of character.

Psychedelic culture has long hoped these substances were a shortcut to a better world: dissolve enough egos and cruelty gives way to empathy. The evidence is mixed at best. People have profound experiences, feel connected to everything, and go back to work on Monday largely unchanged. Insight, it turns out, is not the same as the ability to act under pressure. What matters is not dissolving the ego, a term explained below, but the trust of its parts in the Self to lead.

So what can drugs offer a religion? To answer, we need to know what the Self is and what gets in its way.

## Psychology Basics

These ideas come from Internal Family Systems (IFS), a form of therapy developed by Richard Schwartz and taught by the [IFS Institute](https://ifs-institute.com).

**Parts.** The mind is not one voice. We all have *parts*: an inner critic, a planner, a frightened child, a protector who stashes food. Each has its own feelings and goals, and each is trying to help. Together, the parts are what people usually call the *ego*. Beneath the parts is the *Self*, a calm, curious, compassionate presence. Blended parts crowd it out, but it is always there to return to. *Self-leadership* means letting the Self, not a blended part, decide how you act. The parts are not the enemy. With Self-leadership, they can do their jobs in harmony.

**Blending and unblending.** When a part *blends* with you, you stop noticing it and start being it. You don't think "a part of me is afraid." You just are afraid, and the fear's view of the world is the only view. *Unblending* is stepping back so the part is something you can notice and talk to.

**Mental injury.** When an experience feels too threatening for the whole mind to bear, the mind *splits*. The part that felt the experience is cut off from the rest. Other parts may work hard to keep it locked away. A child punished for anger may exile the anger. A teenager teased for crying may exile their sadness.

The exiled feelings don't vanish. They leak out as tension, sudden rage, anxiety, or a vague sense that something is missing. Healing means meeting the exile with the Self present, which frees its protectors too.

## Entheogens

No drug that mimics the split of mental injury has a place in our practice. Such drugs produce behavior that the rest of the mind neither directs nor remembers, much like a part cut off from the whole. That is why we have no interest in datura[^datura] or in sleep aids like zolpidem.[^zolpidem] A sacrament should bring you closer to your own mind and to the people around you. We see three opportunities for a sacrament:

**Personal emotional healing.** Exiled parts can be met and heard while the rest of the mind stays present. What was split off can rejoin. Kykeon can facilitate this process.

**The circle.** People who have been vulnerable together know one another differently. Kykeon, as an empathogen, can serve here too.

**A test of character.** The most important application. Leaders hold power over others, so the whole community depends on their character. Kykeon is the wrong cup for this. It needs a different class of substance, as explained below.

## An Uncheatable Test of Character

Knowing what's right and doing it under pressure are different things. Therapists feed their own need to be needed. Judges sentence from irritation. Police escalate because adrenaline is doing the thinking. Parents pass their unprocessed trauma to their children. Each holds power over the vulnerable. What's missing is Self-leadership: noticing what you feel and choosing your response instead of letting the feeling drive.

Self-leadership is easy to claim and hard to verify, so the question is how to measure it. Questionnaires capture vocabulary, not behavior. A trained observer's judgment is slow, expensive, and varies between observers. Daily life provokes at random, never the same way twice.

A better test would reliably stir up your own material and watch what happens next. Classical psychedelics such as N,N-DMT, psilocybin, and LSD do this, which is also why bad trips happen. Other drug classes fail. Dissociatives such as ketamine detach you from the experience. Empathogens such as MDMA and kykeon soothe the provocation. Stimulants such as cocaine manufacture confidence instead of testing it.

The psychedelic dose is measured, but what it stirs up is not. Fear, grandiosity, or greed may surge. Either you blend with it and are swept away, or you remain unblended and hold firm.[^firmeza] The outcome depends on how you meet it, which is what makes it a test. Because you stay present and remembering,[^dose] being swept away is a failure of unblending, not a loss of agency. The test can be repeated, so one stumble does not settle anything. Fail once and it might be the occasion. Fail repeatedly and it's you.

Taking the test is voluntary. Those who aspire to lead can distinguish themselves by taking it. Declining carries no penalty, but it forfeits that distinction. The circle witnesses the result and may weigh the difference.

## The Role of Kykeon

The test demands courage: material can ambush you while failure is witnessed. Courage like that needs a bedrock of trust.

Kykeon showers our parts with grace. Held in grace, parts can develop trust in the Self. Shared in a circle, kykeon also begins the bond of vulnerability that holds a community together. It is the church's welcome: an ancient cup offered at the door to all.

A drug alone does not make a religion. Our shared purpose is the test of character. Kykeon prepares us for it.

## Mutual Dependency

The test and the wider political order need each other. The test is open to anyone who holds or seeks power over others. The church is the proving ground, not the throne. The wider order is where leaders serve.

Politics safeguards religious freedom: without laws and officials who protect the right to gather, worship, and use sacraments, no church survives. But politics is only as trustworthy as the people who hold power. Leaders whose Self-leadership has held firm are better candidates for that trust. In turn, they protect the freedom that makes the test possible. See [Incorruptible Institutions Need Incorruptible People First](https://brambles.joshuapritikin.com/posts/incorruptible-and-the-missing-mechanism/).

## Celebration

The Second Church of the Kykeon celebrates healing, which prepares us for the test of character, and community, which makes the test's results something we can act on.
