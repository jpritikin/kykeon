## Drugs

Using drugs is a human right.[^hart] But religious use asks for more: a shared purpose, a practice, and a community that holds its leaders to account.

Psychedelic culture has long hoped these substances were a shortcut to a better world: dissolve enough egos and cruelty gives way to empathy. The evidence is mixed at best. People have profound experiences, feel connected to everything, and go back to work on Monday largely unchanged. Insight, it turns out, is not the same as the ability to act under pressure. What matters is not dissolving the ego but letting the Self lead it.

So what can a drug offer a religion? To answer, we need to know what the Self is and what gets in its way.

## Psychology Basics

These ideas come from Internal Family Systems (IFS), a form of therapy developed by Richard Schwartz and taught by the [IFS Institute](https://ifs-institute.com).

**Parts.** The mind is not one voice. We all have *parts*: an inner critic, a planner, a frightened child, a protector who stashes food. Each has its own feelings and goals, and each is trying to help. The parts that run daily life, the planner and the protector among them, are what people usually call the *ego*. Beneath the parts is the *Self*, a calm, curious, compassionate presence. Blended parts crowd it out, but it is always there to return to. *Self-leadership* means letting the Self, not a blended part, decide how you act. The parts are not the enemy. With Self-leadership, they keep their jobs but stop driving.

**Blending and unblending.** When a part *blends* with you, you stop noticing it and start being it. You don't think "a part of me is afraid." You just are afraid, and the fear's view of the world is the only view. *Unblending* is stepping back so the part is something you can notice and talk to.

**Mental injury.** When an experience feels too threatening for the whole mind to bear, the mind *splits*. The part that felt the experience is cut off from the rest. Other parts may work hard to keep it locked away. A child punished for anger may exile the anger. A teenager teased for crying may exile their sadness.

The exiled feelings don't vanish. They leak out as tension, sudden rage, anxiety, or a vague sense that something is missing. Healing means meeting the exile with the Self present, which frees its guards too.

## Entheogens

No drug that mimics the split of mental injury has a place in our practice. That is why we have no interest in datura[^datura] or in sleep aids like zolpidem.[^zolpidem] Both produce behavior that the rest of the mind neither directs nor remembers, much like a part cut off from the whole. A sacrament should bring you closer to your own mind and to the people around you. We see three opportunities:

**Personal emotional healing.** Exiled parts can be met and heard while the rest of the mind stays present. What was split off can rejoin. Kykeon serves this purpose.

**Social connection and cohesion.** People who have been vulnerable together know one another differently. That bond is what lets a community hold its members to account, and what makes the test's results something it can act on.

**A test of character.** The most important application. Drugs, taken repeatedly, can reveal who stays firm under pressure, so a community can know which leaders to trust. Leaders hold power over others, and the whole community depends on their character.

## An Uncheatable Test of Character

Knowing what's right and doing it under duress are different things. Therapists feed their own need to be needed. Judges sentence from irritation. Police escalate because adrenaline is doing the thinking. Parents pass their unprocessed trauma to their children. Each holds power over the vulnerable. What's missing is Self-leadership: noticing what you feel and choosing your response instead of blending with the part feeling it.

So the special opportunity is measurement, not insight: a way to measure Self-leadership. Questionnaires capture vocabulary, not behavior. A trained observer's judgment is slow, expensive, and varies between observers. Daily life provokes unpredictably, so it cannot serve as a standardized measure.

The test needs a classical psychedelic such as N,N-DMT, psilocybin, or LSD. These intensify inner experience, which is why bad trips happen. Dissociatives such as ketamine are unsuitable because they detach you from the experience. Empathogens such as MDMA are unsuitable because they soothe the provocation. Stimulants such as cocaine are unsuitable because they manufacture confidence instead of testing it.

The dose of a classical psychedelic is measured, but what it stirs up is not. Rehearsed behavior is easy to perform, but these substances can ambush you with material you cannot rehearse. Fear, grandiosity, or greed may surge. Either you blend with it and are swept away, or you remain unblended and hold firm.[^firmeza] The outcome depends on how you meet it, which is what makes it a test. Unlike datura or zolpidem, a classical psychedelic leaves you present and remembering, so being swept away is a failure of unblending, not a loss of agency. The test is repeated, so one stumble does not settle anything. Fail once and it might be the occasion. Fail repeatedly and it's you.

Taking the test is voluntary. Those who aspire to lead can distinguish themselves by taking it. Declining costs nothing except that distinction. A community that can see who stays firm under pressure can choose better leaders.

## The Role of Kykeon

Facing the test takes real bravery. Few people will begin there.

Kykeon is our everyday sacrament. It is gentler than a classical psychedelic and does not provoke the way the test requires. It heals our parts and builds the confidence to unblend long before anyone faces the test. Shared in a circle, it also begins the bond of vulnerability that holds a community together. It is the church's welcome too: an ancient cup raised at the door for all.

A drug alone does not make a religion. MDMA is a therapy adjunct in a clinic and a party favor at a rave. Our shared purpose is the test of character. Kykeon heals us and prepares us for it.

## Mutual Dependency

The test and the wider political order need each other. Politics safeguards religious freedom: without laws and officials who protect the right to gather, worship, and use sacraments, no church survives. But politics is only as trustworthy as the people who hold power. Leaders whose Self-leadership has held under the test are the ones worth trusting with that power. In turn, they protect the freedom that makes the test possible. See [Incorruptible Institutions Need Incorruptible People First](https://brambles.joshuapritikin.com/posts/incorruptible-and-the-missing-mechanism/).

## Celebration

The Second Church of the Kykeon celebrates healing, which makes us ready to be vulnerable together, and community, which makes the test's results something we can act on. The test of character is the purpose the other two serve: it lets the community trust its leaders, who in turn protect the freedom that makes all three possible.
