title: Why Are Drugs Important to Religion?
---

Kykeon Church exists to celebrate a chain of reasoning. It runs from the simple question of why anyone should use drugs at all to the hope of leaders who cannot be bought. Each link below leads to the next.

## 1. A human right, but not the whole story

Carl Hart makes the case in *Drug Use for Grown-Ups* that using drugs is a basic human right of adults. That is reason enough to leave people alone. It is not reason enough for a church to care.

Religious use asks for more. A drug that helps you escape yourself has no place in the sanctuary. That is why we have no interest in datura or in sleep aids like Ambien, which blunt or erase experience. A sacrament should bring you closer to your own mind and to the people around you.

## 2. A few ideas, briefly

These come from Internal Family Systems (IFS), a form of therapy, and are developed at length in my book, *Religion Unburdened by Belief*.

**Parts.** The mind is not one voice. We all have *parts*: an inner critic, a planner, a frightened child, a protector who stashes food. Each has its own feelings and goals, and each is trying to help. Beneath the parts is the *Self*, a calm, curious, compassionate presence that everyone has and nobody can lose.

**Blending and unblending.** When a part *blends* with you, you stop noticing it and start being it. You don't think "a part of me is afraid." You just are afraid, and the fear's view of the world is the only view. *Unblending* is stepping back so the part is something you can notice and talk to. Meditation trains this skill, and so do some drugs.

**Mental injury.** When an experience feels too threatening for the whole mind to bear, the mind *splits*. The part that felt the experience is exiled, cut off from the rest, and other parts work hard to keep it locked away. A child punished for anger may exile the anger. A boy teased for crying may exile his sadness. The exiled feelings don't vanish. They leak out as tension, sudden rage, anxiety, or a vague sense that something is missing. The parts guarding the exile are trapped too, unable to relax. Datura is a stark example. It brings terror, vivid hallucinations that feel completely real, and amnesia afterward. The experience is too overwhelming to integrate, so it can end up walled off, and that is escape, not exploration.

## 3. What psychology offers

*This section is a sketch. Details and citations to come.*

**Personal emotional healing and development.** Classical psychedelics are being studied for work that looks backward (processing old wounds and trauma) and forward (growth in openness, resilience, and purpose).

**Social connection and cohesion.** Shared rites bind people together. Communities that undergo a demanding experience side by side tend to trust one another more.

**An uncheatable test of character.** Self-report can be faked, and rehearsed behavior is easy to perform. A psychedelic experience ambushes you with material you cannot rehearse. Fear, grandiosity, or greed may surge. Either you blend with it and get swept away, or you unblend and hold steady. Fail once and it might be the occasion. Fail consistently and it's you. This matters for leadership: a community that can see who stays steady under pressure can choose better leaders. I developed the idea further in [Incorruptible Institutions Need Incorruptible People First](https://brambles.joshuapritikin.com/posts/incorruptible-and-the-missing-mechanism/). The test only works with consent. It must never become a condition of holding office.

## 4. Religion and politics depend on each other

Religion and politics are mutually dependent, but the dependence is lopsided. Religion is less embedded in politics than politics is embedded in religion. Politics draws its legitimacy, its sense of duty, and its picture of a good person from religious sources. Religion can survive without politics, but politics without something sacred underneath it tends to drift toward pure self-interest.

## 5. Whole-community traditions

Santo Daime offers its sacrament, Daime, even to toddlers. We appreciate the principle behind it: the sacred belongs to the whole community, not only to a select few adults. We note it with respect and do not claim to have a policy on it. Kykeon Church is for consenting adults.

## Where Kykeon fits

Put the links together. Drugs that bring us closer to our own minds, rather than letting us escape them, can heal old injuries, build bonds between people, and show who stays steady under provocation. Communities that can see that steadiness can choose incorruptible leaders. Religion is the natural home for this work because it holds sacred what politics depends on.

That is the solution Kykeon Church celebrates. The Eleusinian mysteries were an ancient version of it: a shared rite, taken in community, that sent people home changed. We take the kykeon as our emblem.
